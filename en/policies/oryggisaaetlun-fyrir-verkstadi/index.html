Site safety plan.

Safe planning of temporary work areas

On every site, people must know where work is taking place, who is in charge, what hazards are present and how to respond to the unexpected. This document describes the procedure for preparing a site-specific safety plan for groundwork, utility excavation and surface finishing. It is a general description and does not replace the health and safety plan prepared specifically for each job.

Responsibility and coordination

Before work begins, the person in charge, the client's contact and how the work of other contractors will be coordinated must be defined. Staff need clear information about the work area, access routes, communication, emergency numbers and who has the authority to stop the work. Near the public or traffic, signs, fencing and traffic management must meet the relevant requirements.

Machinery, driving and access

Pedestrian traffic is separated from machine movements as far as possible. Blind spots, reversing, lifting, overhangs and the layout of access routes are assessed. Work areas must be marked off so that members of the public cannot accidentally walk into a machine's working area. Ground bearing capacity, side slopes, loose soil and the distance to trench edges matter when positioning machines.

Soil, trenches and utility lines

Information on pipes and cables is confirmed and the owners' instructions followed before digging. The risk of trench collapse is assessed in light of depth, soil, water and loads near the edges. Before anyone enters a trench, appropriate battering, shoring, access and an emergency exit are assessed. Never assume that unknown layers are safe, even if the surface looks stable.

Weather and emergency response

Wind, ice, rain, frost, running water and poor visibility may require a different method or a halt to work. The plan covers first aid, evacuation, response to damage to utility lines and access for the emergency services. When the work area changes, the safety arrangements are reviewed again. Staff must know the plan before work begins.

Responsibility and review

The managing director is responsible for this document and for making sure its content is presented to everyone working on the company's behalf. It is reviewed when there are significant changes to the work, equipment or rules, after incidents, and otherwise regularly in line with the scale of the business.

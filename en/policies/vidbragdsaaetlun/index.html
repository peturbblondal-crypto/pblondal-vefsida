In groundwork an emergency can develop quickly, for example a trench collapse, a traffic accident, a cut power cable, a hot water leak, an injured worker or oil pollution. P. Blöndal ehf. ensures that responsibilities and first actions are clear before work begins. Job-specific information on location, access and contacts must be available on site; this document describes the general response rules.

General emergency response

The first steps are to stop work, assess the danger and call 112 if life, health or public safety is at risk. Give clear information about the location, the nature of the incident, the number of injured and access to the scene. Do not enter an unsecured trench to carry out a rescue or approach a damaged power cable without instructions from the emergency services; doing so can cause further accidents. First aid is given within the limits of training and safety.

Damage to utility lines

If power, hot water, cold water or sewer lines are damaged, cordon off the area, keep people away from the danger and notify the utility immediately. Where there is a suspected electrical hazard, never assume that a cable is dead. With a hot water leak, avoid steam, jets of water and areas where burns could occur. Work does not resume until the operator or another responsible party has confirmed that it is safe.

Pollution incidents

In the event of a fuel or hydraulic oil leak, stop the source if it is safe to do so and prevent the substance from reaching drains or water. Use suitable absorbents and protective equipment. The extent, location and type of substance are assessed and the relevant parties notified when the rules require it. Contaminated absorbents and soil are handled as appropriate waste according to the assessment and the instructions of the receiving facility.

After an incident

The person in charge ensures that the necessary notifications are made, secures the scene as needed and communicates with the client. Once the immediate danger has passed, the cause is assessed, repairs decided and the risk assessment reviewed. Staff are told which responses were right and what could be improved. For jobs at remote sites, phone coverage, exact location and emergency response times are checked before work begins.

Responsibility and review

The managing director is responsible for this document and for making sure its content is presented to everyone working on the company's behalf. It is reviewed when there are significant changes to the work, equipment or rules, after incidents, and otherwise regularly in line with the scale of the business.

Procedure 17

Onboarding and safety training.

A safe start and continuous training

A new employee, casual helper or subcontractor may have good experience but does not automatically know the equipment, methods and risks of a particular site. Onboarding at P. Blöndal ehf. ensures that each person knows their role, who is in charge of the job, who to talk to and when to stop work. Onboarding is a process in which skills and understanding are verified, not just a handover of instructions.

Introduction to the company and the job

Before work begins, we go through the role, the site, lines of communication, rights under the contract and expectations. The new person receives information about the safety culture, the code of conduct, personal protective equipment and rules for dealing with clients. Particular emphasis is placed on making it clear that raising safety concerns, unclear drawings or problems is never considered a disruption to progress.

Site safety and equipment

The work area, access routes, danger zones around machines, open trenches and the location of emergency equipment are shown. We go through methods for digging near pipes and cables, protection against collapse, pedestrian traffic and communication between the operator and the banksman. The new person receives instructions on the correct use of equipment, and licences are checked before they are given control of a machine or other specialist work.

Training and supervision

Practical demonstrations are given when needed. A newcomer does not work independently on high-risk tasks until the person in charge has verified sufficient skill and safety awareness. We do not assume that everyone has the same native language or the same experience; information is presented so that the person understands it. If tasks or equipment change, training is repeated or extended.

Communication and follow-up

After the first jobs, we discuss whether instructions were clear, whether the equipment was suitable and whether any hazards were underestimated. Feedback from newcomers can reveal unclear procedures that more experienced staff no longer notice. Training is kept up with short conversations when new risks arise, while formal training and confirmation of licences follow the requirements in force.

Responsibility and review

The managing director is responsible for this document and for making sure its content is presented to everyone working on the company's behalf. It is reviewed when there are significant changes to the work, equipment or rules, after incidents, and otherwise regularly in line with the scale of the business.

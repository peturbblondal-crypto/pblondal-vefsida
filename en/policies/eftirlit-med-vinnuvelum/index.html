Daily inspection of machinery.

Machines are key equipment in groundwork but also a source of risk if their condition is inadequate. P. Blöndal ehf. carries out preventive daily checks on its machines, based on the manufacturer's instructions, mandatory inspections and the conditions of each job. The aim is to find faults before they cause an accident, breakdown or pollution, not to create unnecessary paperwork.

Before starting a machine

The operator checks the general condition, loose guards, leaks, wheels or tracks, the bucket and attachments, hoses and access to the machine. The hydraulic system, oil and fluid levels are checked according to the manufacturer's instructions. Seat belts, doors, mirrors, lights, warning devices and the necessary protective systems must also be checked. Only a competent person may put a machine into use.

Function and working environment

Before full work begins, the normal function of controls, brakes, warning systems and relevant locks is confirmed. The ground, slope, stability and blind spots are checked. If people on foot are working near the machine, a safe division of tasks and communication are needed. When attachments are used, correct fitting and weight and operating limits are verified according to the manufacturer's manual.

Responding to faults

If a fault could endanger people or the environment, the machine is taken out of use and steps are taken to ensure it cannot be used by mistake until it has been assessed as safe. Minor issues also need clear handling and a time set for repair. Safety switches must never be bypassed or warning devices disconnected to keep work going. In the event of an oil leak, pollution control measures are taken and the need to report it is assessed.

Maintenance and responsibility

The owner or person responsible for a machine ensures regular maintenance and statutory inspections as applicable. The operator reports faults and any abnormal behaviour of the machine. Repairs that require specialist knowledge are carried out by qualified people. Records of maintenance and repairs are kept in line with the company's operations. For each machine, the relevant manufacturer's instructions are used rather than a single general checklist. Daily checks do not replace statutory machine inspections, the service manual or the operator's licence.

Responsibility and review

The managing director is responsible for this document and for making sure its content is presented to everyone working on the company's behalf. It is reviewed when there are significant changes to the work, equipment or rules, after incidents, and otherwise regularly in line with the scale of the business.
